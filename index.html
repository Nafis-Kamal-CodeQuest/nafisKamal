What am I still trying to figure out?

Ongoing research, proposal stage, with two coauthors

Beyond the semantic shortcut: does a fix for vision-language models hold up in the real world?

Vision-language models can fall into a failure called semantic anchoring, and a recent fine-tuning fix (Shahgir et al., 2026) addresses it. Our study asks whether that fix still holds up on fine-grained, real-world problems. The plan is to begin with a small model, then scale to larger ones using LoRA.

Vision-language modelsFine-tuningLoRA
Ongoing capstone research, team of four, moving into data collection

Where should a nationwide delivery network put its warehouses?

A last-mile delivery system for all of Bangladesh, modeled on a large operator headquartered in Dhaka that handles thousands of deliveries a day. The study asks how many new warehouses to add beyond the Dhaka hub, where they matter most, and how solar-charged electric trucks should be routed. It optimizes for cost and distance travelled, not time alone, and pairs route optimization with a simulation of nationwide demand.

What makes code worth reading?

Programs must be written for people to read, and only incidentally for machines to execute. Abelson and Sussman, Structure and Interpretation of Computer Programs

I care less about making something run than about knowing why it runs, and how it could run better. Readable, maintainable, purposeful code is what I aim for.

I’m drawn to the place where algorithms meet product thinking, where a clean abstraction solves a real person’s problem. Outside projects, I practise algorithms, study system design, and look for ways software can be both elegant and fast.
